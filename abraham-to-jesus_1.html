David's two sons
Why are there two genealogies from here?

Matthew traces David's line through Solomon (the throne); Luke traces it through Nathan, another son of David. Tap for the harmonization.

Matthew 1 and Luke 3 agree completely from Abraham to David, then split. Matthew follows Solomon — the royal, legal line of succession to the throne. Luke follows Nathan, a different son of David entirely. The two lines run in parallel for roughly nine hundred years and then meet again at Shealtiel and Zerubbabel around the exile, before splitting a second time and finally converging at Joseph.

The traditional harmonizations offered by early Christian writers (e.g. Julius Africanus, and later Eusebius) generally take one of two approaches: either Luke gives Mary's biological ancestry while Matthew gives Joseph's legal/royal line (making Jesus a true "son of David" both legally through Joseph and by blood through Mary), or both genealogies trace Joseph but through different mechanisms — including a levirate marriage that would make one line biological and the other legal at a specific generation. Scripture doesn't settle which explanation is correct; both were considered live options by the earliest interpreters.
